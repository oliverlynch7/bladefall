# Black Woods signal approach — grounded change packet

The live Black Woods has two valid ways to silence the Legion horn: fight through the camp to its ground crank, or climb from the eastern roots and cut the upper cable. Lewis describes that choice clearly. The upper route does not yet deliver the promised traversal: its 9 + 11 + 14 broad slabs overlap into one continuous elevated walkway. A normal-camera inspection shows long anonymous gray surfaces rather than a readable tree-supported route. The optional BR-03 loft branches from that walkway and is likewise a continuous stair. The signal deck and story flags already work and should remain.

Replace only the eastern elevated path with separated, collision-backed perches. Begin with a short rise that teaches the spacing, vary the next jump directions and heights, and let the safe forest floor catch misses. Give each platform visible timber, brackets and a warm rope/marker line that points toward the signal horn. The upper and ground routes must meet at the same deck, where the existing cable or crank opens `woods.signal`. Make the horn a persistent landmark without hiding either control or the player. The optional loft to BR-03 should use a few tighter jumps, but stay reachable with ordinary movement and one jump. This is a geometry and presentation pass, not a new quest or another input puzzle.

Preserve Lewis, Beth's rescue, the separate animal-track root secret, all BR shard IDs, the ground approach, `woods.ready`, `woods.signal`, co-op story authority, checkpoint behavior and older saves. Do not make the upper route required. Avoid transparent floor art, overlapping invisible collision, z-fighting and a tower that blocks the normal camera. Test from the forest floor with real movement: climb, miss/retry, upper cable, optional loft shard and ordinary ground crank. Inspect normal-camera frames from the start, mid-route and tower; then run the complete Black Woods route, old-save, co-op, story and syntax checks. Automated traversal establishes reachability, not fun; Oliver's human playtest remains the final judgment of challenge and art.
