## Narrative promise

You leave home because waiting for rescue has become another way to surrender. At first you save a few people and open a road. Those small victories help others resist. Following the Legion’s supplies and orders brings you closer to the people behind the Hollow Gates, then to proof of what happened to their victims. The King begins preparing for you only after your victories reach him. Your identity is confirmed in the final fight, then by Ian inside the Void.

The useful question behind each main task is: what does this change for the people here, and why does it lead toward stopping the King? Optional quests may be personal, funny or strange, but must leave a clear result.

## Ending

King defeated -> control anchor fails -> binding destabilizes -> player and battlefield drawn into Void -> Ian’s spirit confirms the player -> temporary Ian’s Blade -> timed Space-bar charge -> final cut severs binding -> all Hollowed restored, including destroyed bodies -> player escapes and Aerth has another chance.

Failure retries only the charge. After five consecutive failures, show Skip challenge. Skipping leads into the same successful cut, without shaming the player or reducing rewards. The permanent shop version of Ian’s Blade remains separate. The exact explanation for not retaining the temporary blade stays open.

## Story delivered through play

Use a repeating purpose, not repeating tasks: help someone act, change a place, learn why the Legion is there, choose the next move. Change the activity between levels: medicine delivery, route repair, convoy interception, defense, escape, investigation, infiltration and confrontation. Do not turn every area into three fetched parts and a lever.

Legion documents communicate practical orders rather than a convenient full villain diary. Historical posters use Eternal Gate; survivors use Hollow Gate. Independent beasts remain independent unless deliberately assigned otherwise. Hostile Hollowed cannot freely betray commands; helpful living NPCs can.

## Hub conversations and recording scope

Initial shop introductions are short, permanently remembered per save, and never prerequisites for repeated shopping. New story conversations get a visible marker and a separate What has changed? option. Player can still choose the shop immediately.

- Quartermaster: dry humor; explains buying and selling, later requests a missing supply ledger tied to refugees.
- Smith: blunt, practical; explains equipment services, later recognizes parts from Gate-building shipments without claiming knowledge of the whole binding.
- Beastkeeper: warm and excitable; explains active companions, later reacts to discovered secret companions. Undiscovered companions stay hidden in the menu.
- Keeper: composed; explains appearance services. Later offers a personal quest concerning clothing recovered from an abandoned village; no disguise-magic canon is invented.
- Drillmaster: stern with occasional deadpan jokes; explains class trials and compatible weapons, later notices technique and persistence rather than announcing ancestry.
- Rift assembly role: assign to a suitable existing hub NPC after inspecting its UI and workload; do not create a new magical origin for Secret Rifts merely to explain this menu.

Record by character, then scene. Most choices have two or three options, one distinct response and a shared continuation. Important branches affect access, assistance, rewards or world state. Avoid exponential branching and avoid choices that secretly block the required route. Stable line IDs survive wording edits; approved audio must be flagged stale if spoken wording changes.
