BLADEFALL · CAMPAIGN DESIGN LAB · PROPOSAL 01 · 4 OCTOBER 2026

Ruined Keep
The lift rescue

A focused redesign of the campaign dungeon rescue. Prove that a place can be understandable, satisfying to explore, and worth saving people from—before rebuilding the rest of the campaign.

Status: design for review, not approved new canon or implemented gameplay.
Existing levels, saves, dialogue and rewards remain untouched. The approved direction is an isolated beta and map-first design. All dimensions, timings, puzzle states and encounter details below are proposals.
MapsPlayer journeyPuzzleCombat & escortPlacement rulesBeta acceptance

01 / What this beta must prove

Player promise: “I can see who needs help, understand why they cannot leave, figure out the machinery, and get them safely out.” The rescue should emerge from the building. It should not feel like a checklist pasted onto unrelated scenery.

  • One compact, connected rescue area; proposed first-time target of 15–25 minutes, not a requirement to pad travel.
  • One spatial mechanism with a visible consequence; one moderate traversal route; one short protected evacuation.
  • A small optional discovery tied to a real person. No new roster of quest dispensers.
  • Return travel changes purpose: the preparation route becomes an escorted route, not another fetch-and-return.

02 / Existing story we keep

Broken Walls establishes a prisoner rescue and points into the cells. Walter knows the prison; Sly has stolen a family keepsake and a purple shard. Records and Walter lead the player toward Ellis in Frostfell. The Fallen stands beyond the upper shelter. The keep is a holding location, not another Hollow Gate site.

The live script currently says “Use the eastern service stairs to follow them.” That instruction and its accessible staircase undermine the lift objective. The beta removes the competing route physically, rather than inventing a warning that tells prisoners not to use it.

In this slice
Lower holding rooms, guard station, lift machinery, service access, refuge. Walter and Sly retain their roles.
Outside this slice
Broken Walls redesign, full Fallen arena, all five campaign shard placements, final reward balance, new canon or permanent voice recordings.

03 / Maps and spatial contract

These diagrams define connectivity and separation by height. They are not a terrain-generation seed. The eventual construction plan must add measured widths, cover heights, sightlines and tested jump distances.

Non-negotiable geometry: no side staircase, ladder, decorative ramp or jumpable roof may connect the lower cells to the refuge. E–F ends at the machinery. No invisible wall should be needed to preserve this distinction.

05 / The lift puzzle: position creates a route

One rule: the lift and its counterweight move in opposite directions. A walkable maintenance cage sits on the weight. The cage becomes a temporary bridge only when aligned with the service ledges.

Winch positionWhat changesPurpose
Lower dockLift low, counterweight high.Prisoners can board; service gap remains open.
Service stopLift and cage meet their middle marks.The cage bridges E to F. Its landing outlines visibly align.
Upper dockLift high, counterweight low.Reaches G, but the visible snag initially prevents this movement.

Solution: align the cage at the service stop → climb and cross → remove the snag → return → lower for boarding → raise to the refuge. This is a spatial observation puzzle, not arithmetic or a hidden button sequence.

What makes it a puzzle: the game shows movement and alignment without immediately naming the correct setting. The player must recognize that the counterweight is also a crossing. If the prototype feels like simply cycling three buttons until one works, revise it before adding detail. Do not call it solved design yet.

  • Controls show a small physical lift diagram and current stop. No long journal explanation is required.
  • At first, one short optional Walter hint: “The weight moves opposite the lift.” A later requested hint can mention the maintenance cage.
  • Camera remains controllable; a brief optional focus shows the chain after the first failed upper-dock attempt.
  • Movement is locked while a player occupies the service cage or crosses its threshold. Show “Someone is using the service bridge.” Never let a co-op partner drop them unexpectedly.
  • The lift must not move until boarding gates are clear. Include the player and companions in occupancy checks.
  • Falls return the player to a nearby stable ledge; no puzzle reset and no repeat dialogue. Damage, if any, is a tuning question.

06 / Combat and a short escort

Encounter 1 — guard station: one shield-bearing guard controls the direct doorway; a ranged guard uses the desk-side lane. A side aisle gives a flank. Cover breaks line of sight without letting the player attack through a wall. Enemies occupy work and guard positions rather than an evenly spaced grid.

Encounter 2 — evacuation: after the player explicitly starts the escort, reinforcements enter through an existing guard passage at B. They do not materialize beside the prisoners. Start with one clear threat, then add a flanker if the first playtest supports it. No endless spawns.

Escort proposal: use one designated wounded captive with a visible health bar; the other survivors follow as a group. This concentrates attention rather than asking the player to monitor four bars. Pause/resume at safe stops; the group never walks through an active fight just because its timer advanced.

On failure, restore the escort-start encounter snapshot: captive health, enemy state, player resources and reward eligibility. Keep the solved lift puzzle and discovered story. This prevents repeated rewards or farming while avoiding a full-level restart. Solo must be fully playable; co-op may divide protection and machinery duties without requiring two players.

07 / Optional content that belongs here

Sly’s restitution: keep the established stolen locket and shard story. Put the owner’s identifiable bundle in C, near the person it belongs to. Let the player return it during preparation, and let the resolution occur at the refuge. Remove the unrelated trip back to a distant quest marker. Preserve meaningful dialogue choices; do not make confession or forgiveness a prerequisite for the main rescue.

Exploration reward: one harder maintenance alcove, visible from E, may hold a temporary beta reward. It uses the same architectural logic and adds a demanding optional jump. Its final item and shard assignment remain open. The beta does not award live Reaper unlock progress.

No extra filler: no scattered tools quest, duplicate pressure-plate puzzle, or chest at every turn. Optional content must deepen this place or ask for a different skill.

08 / Placement and visual rules

AssetPlacement reasonReject if…
Crates and cartsAt receiving and loading points; clear cart-width travel between them.They are sprinkled around to fill space or block a working lift.
RubbleUnder a visibly broken wall or stair, with matching material.There is no source of collapse, or it looks climbable but has no collision.
Chains, wheels, bracesTrace a coherent load path from winch to lift and counterweight.A decorative chain ends in empty space or implies a false puzzle.
LightWarmer light at occupied guard areas; readable service machinery; a clear refuge landmark.Darkness hides essential interaction cues or fake doors compete with the real exit.
Personal belongingsSpecific beds, confiscation shelves and bundles; few, legible clusters.They imply collectible loot but cannot be inspected, or contradict the rescue.

Use large shapes and landmarks first. Only after they work: structural detail, evidence of use, then restrained wear and debris. Keep the current asset language for this prototype; it is not a commitment to a new global visual identity.

09 / Concise communication

Suggested tracker text, one objective at a time:

  1. Free the prisoners.
  2. Restore the cargo lift.
  3. Bring the prisoners to the lift.
  4. Reach the upper refuge.

Keep puzzle answers out of automatic objective arrows. Arrows can identify the winch room or missing prisoners, not the correct stop. Journal entries are a fallback, with a current-clue card for this area. Dialogue is optional except for essential choices; never hide an available side topic after the main conversation.

10 / Isolated beta and acceptance gates

Proposed build boundary: a separate preview deployment and “Campaign Design Lab” entry. Dedicated disposable test state, no campaign-save writes, no permanent gold/gear/shard rewards. Do not reuse the normal campaign checkpoint namespace. Include reset, exit and a visible BETA label. A copied test character is a one-way import, never written back.

GateEvidence needed before proceeding
Map reviewEach route has a physical purpose. No ordinary alternate route invalidates the lift. Lower/service/upper connections are unambiguous.
Plain geometryFresh Warrior, Ranger and Mage can traverse mandatory routes. Every jump is measured with real game physics; no class teleport required.
Puzzle prototypeA new player can observe movement, attempt a hypothesis and recognize the result. Repeated random control cycling is a redesign signal.
Rescue loopEscort starts deliberately, can pause safely, takes visible damage and retries without replaying setup or duplicating rewards.
Solo/co-op reliabilityNo moving-bridge griefing, trapped riders, disconnect deadlocks or duplicate outcomes. Pause, death, respawn and rejoin have defined states.
First visual passProps explain use, landmarks orient the player, platforms remain visible, all intended solids collide, all camera modes work.
Human playtestObserve without explaining. Ask the player what the goal was, why the lift was needed, and which moment was satisfying. Do not equate completion with enjoyment.

Observe, don’t over-instrument: note where the player stops, loops, guesses, misses a cue or tries a plausible unsupported action. Record time spent only travelling versus making decisions. One family playtest is useful evidence, not universal proof. Automated reachability and state tests complement it.

Stop criteria: if the lift is unnecessary, the cage solution is trial-and-error, most time is empty running, or the player needs creator narration, return to the map/puzzle stage. Do not compensate by adding more arrows, dialogue or props.

11 / Build order after review

  1. Lock the room connections and separate beta/save boundary.
  2. Build plain floors, walls, lift, service cage and checkpoints. No decorative pass.
  3. Test movement and camera; correct scale and sightlines.
  4. Implement the observable puzzle and its co-op safety states.
  5. Add the guard encounter, short escort and concise NPC interactions.
  6. Playtest the complete rescue; revise the map as needed.
  7. Add purposeful art and only then consider replacing the campaign section.
Decisions to evaluate in this packet: the counterweight-as-bridge puzzle, one damageable escorted captive, and a beta ending at the refuge. These are concrete proposals, not assumptions to silently carry into production.
